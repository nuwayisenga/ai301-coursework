# Rubric: is this a good first issue?

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| repo-alive | The repo-facts block: the `archived:` flag, `last push to any branch`, and the dates on the `last 5 default-branch commits`, all read against the bundle's `captured:` date. | `archived: no`, AND the last push to any branch is within 90 days of the capture date, AND at least 3 of the last 5 default-branch commits are dated within 180 days of the capture date. | required |
| maintainer-responsive | The repo-facts `maintainer first-response sample (5 recently updated issues, days to first owner/member/collaborator comment)`, and, when that sample can't speak, the `repo-alive` commit dates and authors. | At least 1 of the 5 sampled issues received a first owner/member/collaborator comment within 45 days. If the sample has fewer than 3 entries, or every sampled issue was opened within 7 days of the capture date (too recent to have had time for a response), pass instead if the last 5 default-branch commits include activity within 7 days of capture from more than one contributor — an actively-committed repo substitutes for a response signal the sample is too thin to give. A full sample of 5 where all read "no maintainer comment in thread" fails outright; the substitution does not apply once the sample is large enough to speak. | required |
| unclaimed | The repo-facts `this issue: assignees / linked PRs` line, plus every comment in the bundle's comment thread. | `assignees: none` AND `linked PRs: none` (or every linked PR is closed, not merged) AND no comment from a non-author claiming the issue (e.g. "I'll take this", "working on this now", "PR incoming", a bot's claim-confirmation reply) dated within 90 days of the capture date with no sign of abandonment since. A claim older than 90 days, or one followed by a stale-bot mark, an auto-unassignment, or several months of silence, is abandoned and does not block — evidence the abandonment, don't just note the claim's age. A claim inside the 90-day window, or one with no evidence of lapsing, still blocks. | required |
| bounded-scope | The issue title and body: what single problem or feature it asks for, and whether closing it requires one coherent change versus several genuinely separate ones. | The issue names one problem to fix or one feature to build, landable in a single pull request. Root-cause analysis, named contributing causes, or optional "nice to have" suggestions for that same one problem do not fail this check — only resolving the core reported symptom is required to close the issue. Fails when the body is a checklist of independently shippable subtasks that would each need their own PR to close their share of the issue (a tracker, umbrella, meta, or epic), or when the work necessarily sweeps unrelated areas of the whole codebase (e.g. "add type annotations across the project", "migrate all modules"). Touching several files, or listing several diagnosed causes or stretch suggestions for one bug or feature, still passes. A `good first issue` label does not override this check. | required |
| spec-is-concrete | The issue body: whether it names a definite implementation target, or instead defers a needed design or product choice. | Fails when the body itself marks a needed input as undecided (e.g. "TBD", "not identified", an explicit unresolved design/product question the newcomer would have to decide) with no maintainer comment resolving it. A well-specified request — concrete file, behavior, or acceptance criterion, nothing load-bearing left undecided — passes even with no maintainer comment at all. | required |
| policy-permits-newcomer | The repo-facts `contribution policy` line (from CONTRIBUTING.md or equivalent), read in full. | The stated policy does not bar or gate this contribution for an outside newcomer: no "not accepting external contributions", no core-team-only requirement, no signed CLA or legal agreement required before a PR, no ban on AI-assisted contributions, and no requirement to be assigned the issue before opening a PR. A policy that merely sets style, testing, or disclosure expectations passes. | required |
| solution-located | The issue body and comment thread. | The issue names where the fix goes or what the finished result looks like — a file, module, page, function, error message, or an explicit acceptance criterion — rather than only reporting that something is wrong. | preferred |
| newcomer-signalled | The issue labels in the bundle header, and the author's role annotation (e.g. `MEMBER`, `CONTRIBUTOR`) on the opening post. | The issue carries a `good first issue`, `beginner`, `help wanted`, `documentation`, or equivalent label, or was filed by a maintainer (`OWNER`/`MEMBER`). | preferred |
| discussion-settled | The comment thread. | No unresolved disagreement about whether the change should happen or which approach is right, and no maintainer comment questioning the premise. An empty thread passes. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

Accept if and only if every `required` check grades `pass`.

- A single `required` check grading `fail` produces `reject`.
- `unclear` on a `required` check counts as `fail`. The bundle is the whole
  world in eval mode; if the evidence a required check names is absent from
  it, the issue is not one a newcomer can verify, and it is rejected.
- `preferred` checks never change the verdict. Report their grades, and use
  them to rank accepted issues: an accepted issue with more preferred checks
  passing ranks above one with fewer.
